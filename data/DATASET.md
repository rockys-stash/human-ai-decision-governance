# Datasets

Three decision domains. Two are public tabular datasets committed under `data/raw/`; the third is
generated in code. Every domain is reduced to the same shape (`src/governance/data/domains.py`): a
binary decision where `y = 1` means the positive action is correct, a per-case stake, an asymmetric
cost for each error type, display features, and group attributes used only for error analysis.

| Domain | Source | Rows | Positive action | Base rate of `y = 1` | Stake | Cost false approve : false decline |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `credit` | UCI Statlog German Credit | 1,000 | approve credit | 0.70 | credit amount (DM) | 5 : 1 (dataset's own cost matrix) |
| `eligibility` | UCI Adult (Census Income) | 48,842 | grant eligibility | 0.76 | 1 (unit) | 1 : 3 (assumption) |
| `payments` | synthetic, `make_synthetic()` | 20,000 per seed | approve payment | about 0.88 (seed-dependent) | payment amount | 1 : 0.25 (assumption) |

## UCI Statlog (German Credit Data)

- Original: Hofmann, H. (1994). *Statlog (German Credit Data)*. UCI Machine Learning Repository.
  https://doi.org/10.24432/C5NC77. Licence: CC BY 4.0.
- File: `data/raw/german.csv`, 1,000 rows, 20 attributes plus class, no header, symbolic codes
  (A11, A12, ...). Fetched from the mirror
  `https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jbrownlee/Datasets/master/german.csv` because the UCI site and
  OpenML were unreachable from the build environment. It is byte-for-byte the comma-separated form
  of the UCI `german.data` file; the loader verifies SHA-256
  `ec12a88b9fc14d74ba646ea0410cf7ff4533bec2eb61652f8ad76796bbfec017`.
- Class 1 = good credit (700), class 2 = bad credit (300). Codes are mapped to readable labels
  from the dataset documentation so the review console can show them.
- The documentation's cost matrix (approving a bad applicant costs 5, declining a good one costs
  1) is used as given and multiplied by the credit amount, so a large bad loan is the costliest
  error.
- Group attributes for error analysis: sex (derived from `personal_status_sex`) and age band
  (under 25 / 25 and over). They are **not** removed from the model's features, to mirror how the
  public dataset is typically used; the report breaks errors down by these groups rather than
  claiming fairness.

## UCI Adult (Census Income)

- Original: Becker, B. and Kohavi, R. (1996). *Adult*. UCI Machine Learning Repository.
  https://doi.org/10.24432/C5XW20. Licence: CC BY 4.0.
- File: `data/raw/adult-all.csv`, 48,842 rows (the UCI train and test files combined), 14
  attributes plus income, no header. Fetched from
  `https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jbrownlee/Datasets/master/adult-all.csv`; SHA-256
  `21e0ea2f925a00338929a8c86c27354c72ac1c79819bcca81c7d91c3d64218c2`. Missing values (`?`)
  become the category `unknown`.
- **Framing (a stand-in, stated plainly):** the task is presented as eligibility screening for an
  income-tested programme, so `income <= 50K` is "eligible" (`y = 1`). The label is 1994 census
  income, not a real eligibility outcome. Costs are an assumption: wrongly denying an eligible
  person costs 3, wrongly granting costs 1, with unit stakes. Policies in this domain therefore
  route on expected cost and confidence, not on stake.
- `fnlwgt` (a census sampling weight) is not used as a feature.
- Group attributes: sex and race, for error breakdowns only.

## Synthetic payments

- Generated by `make_synthetic(n=20000, seed, covariate_shift, concept_shift)`. Each payment
  request has amount (log-normal), vendor age, vendor risk score, invoice mismatch, changed bank
  details, after-hours submission and region.
- The probability that a payment is legitimate is a known logistic function of those features
  (`SYNTH_COEF`), and `y` is drawn from it. Because the true probability is known, calibration and
  routing can be checked against ground truth, not just against labels.
- Shift (experiment E5): `covariate_shift` moves mass toward riskier vendors, larger amounts and
  more mismatches without changing the outcome model; `concept_shift` makes changed bank details
  more predictive of fraud, which a model trained before the shift cannot know.
- Costs (assumption): approving a fraudulent payment loses its amount; rejecting a legitimate one
  costs 25% of the amount in handling and supplier friction.

## Splits and leakage controls

- Per seed, each domain is split stratified 60 / 20 / 20 into train, calibration and test
  (`stratified_split`, disjointness asserted by `Split.check_disjoint`).
- The model and encoder see only train rows; calibrators and policy thresholds see only the
  calibration split; every reported metric is on the test split, replayed as a shuffled arrival
  stream.
- Policy thresholds in `configs/policies/` were chosen on tuning seeds 100 to 104, which are
  disjoint from the reported seeds (base 20261008).
- Case difficulty for the simulated reviewer comes from a separate logistic-regression reference
  model trained on the train split (the true probability for `payments`), so reviewer accuracy does
  not depend on the agent being evaluated.

## What is not here

- No real human decisions or reviewer logs. Reviewer behaviour is simulated with stated parameters
  (`ReviewerModel`), and its conclusions are tested for sensitivity in E4. A study with real
  reviewers is **Status: pending** (see `docs/PLAN.md`).
